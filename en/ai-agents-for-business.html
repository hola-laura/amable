AI agents for business: what information they need and how to stop them making things up

An agent does not invent out of malice: it invents when a fact is missing and nobody told it that “I don’t know” is allowed. Most of the work is preparing its information, its permissions and its limits.

In short
  • An assistant answers; an agent also does things: it searches, writes into your tools, sends.
  • It invents when information is missing or when two documents contradict each other. The fix is one up-to-date place for each fact, not a “smarter” model.
  • Give it the minimum: just the data and permissions the task needs. Anything that costs money or goes out to a customer is confirmed by a person.
  • Start with reading, then drafting, then proposing actions. Autonomy is earned one task at a time.

Over the last year the conversation has moved from “do you use AI?” to “do you have agents?”. With it has come a reasonable fear: that the agent tells a customer a price that does not exist, or sends an email nobody has checked.

That fear is not solved by choosing a different tool. It is solved earlier, by what you give the agent and what you let it do.

How is an agent different from an assistant?

AssistantYou ask and it answersIt drafts, summarises, translates. You copy the result and decide what to do with it.
AgentYou give it a job and it does itIt chains steps and uses your tools: it checks the calendar, fills in a sheet, prepares something to send.

That is the difference that matters: the agent acts. So what would be a text mistake in an assistant can be a sent email in an agent.

Why does an AI agent make things up?

A language model is built to complete. If you ask it something it does not have in front of it, it tends to give an answer that sounds right. In a company that mostly happens for three reasons:

  • It does not have the fact. Nobody wrote down anywhere how much the urgent service costs.
  • It has two different facts. The 2025 price list and the 2026 one are still in the same folder.
  • Nobody told it what to do when it does not know. And “I don’t know, let me check” is a perfectly valid answer.

What information does it need?

  1. A context documentWho you are, what you sell, to whom, in what tone and what is never said. One or two pages, not forty.
  2. A single source for each factPrices in one place, opening hours in another, terms in another. Dated, and with a person who keeps it up to date.
  3. Examples of what good looks likeThree real replies you are happy with are worth more than ten instructions.
  4. Permission not to knowA written rule: “if the fact is not in these sources, say so and pass it to a person”.

Notice that none of this is technical. It is putting in order what the company already knows. In fact, it is the same work you need to automate any task with AI: the process first, the tool second.

Which permissions should it have, and where does a person go?

In October 2026 the Spanish Data Protection Agency (AEPD) published a ten-point guide on AI assistants and agents. Its central idea is easy to remember: the agent prepares and the person approves. It also recommends granting only the permissions each task strictly needs, with no permanent authorisations, and asking for confirmation before sending, sharing, paying or signing up to anything.

The same goes for data: the agent should only see what it needs for the task. If it can work without customers’ names or phone numbers, better.

How do you roll it out gradually?

  1. ReadsIt answers the team’s questions from your sources and says where each fact comes from. Nobody outside sees it.
  2. DraftsIt writes drafts (replies, summaries, quotes) that a person reviews and sends.
  3. ProposesIt suggests the action (“shall I send this to Martha?”) and waits for a yes.
  4. ActsIt does alone only the low-risk things it has already done well many times. Everything else stays at stage 3.

Each task moves up a stage when it has gone a few weeks without mistakes, not when the calendar says so. And always with a log of what it has done and a clear way to stop it.

Frequently asked questions

What is an AI agent in a company?

It is an AI system that, as well as answering, chains steps and uses the company’s tools (calendar, sheets, email) to complete a task. The difference from an assistant is that it acts, rather than only drafting.

How do I stop an AI agent from making up facts?

Give it a single up-to-date source for each fact, ask it to say where each answer comes from, and write down the rule that if the fact is not there, it says so and passes it to a person. And test it with real questions before using it.

Can an AI agent email customers without review?

Technically yes, but I do not recommend it at the start. Anything that leaves the company or commits money should be confirmed by a person. The Spanish Data Protection Agency sums it up this way: the agent prepares and the person approves.
