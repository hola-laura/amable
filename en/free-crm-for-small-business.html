Free CRM for small businesses: when a spreadsheet is enough, and when it isn’t

A CRM isn’t expensive software for sales teams. It’s a list of who asked you for something and what happens next. Here’s when a spreadsheet does the job, when a free CRM does, and when neither is enough.

Many sole traders keep track of customers in three places at once: WhatsApp, their inbox and their head. It works until someone says “I messaged you last week” and you have no idea what they mean.

That’s exactly what a CRM solves. And you don’t need to pay for one to start.

What is a CRM, in plain words?

CRM stands for customer relationship management. In practice, it’s a list where everyone who asked you for something has four details: who they are, where things stand, what needs doing now, and when.

If you have that, you have a CRM, even in a notebook. Everything else (reports, automatic emails, dashboards) is extra.

The spreadsheet that works

Six columns. Not one more to begin with, because every extra column is another excuse not to fill it in:

Header row to paste into your sheet

Contact Source Status Next step Date Notes

Two tricks that change everything: status is picked from a drop-down (so you don’t end up with “quote”, “Quote sent” and “qte pending”), and no row ever has an empty next step. If there’s nothing to do, the status is “Won” or “Lost”.

When is a spreadsheet enough?

A sheet is enough if…
  • You work alone, or there are two of you who talk every day
  • A handful of new contacts come in each week
  • Checking the sheet every morning is easy
  • Nobody needs automatic alerts
You’ve outgrown it if…
  • Several of you deal with customers and nobody knows who has whom
  • Follow-ups slip even though the sheet is up to date
  • You want every email with a customer in one place
  • You copy details from forms or emails by hand

There’s no magic number of contacts. The signal is different: when the sheet depends on you remembering to look at it, it’s stopped helping.

HubSpot free: what you get and where it falls short

HubSpot is the best-known free CRM, and its free plan doesn’t expire. According to its help centre, new free accounts can store up to 1,000 contacts and have up to 2 users. Older accounts may have different limits.

The goodEverything in one placeA record for each contact, email history, dated tasks and a board of deals by stage.
The goodForms that save themselvesWhoever fills in your website form goes straight in as a contact, with no copying.
The limitTwo peopleIf there are three of you, the third needs a paid plan.
The limitAutomations cost extraSequences and the most useful automatic workflows are on the paid plans.

Other CRMs have free plans too (Zoho, Notion with a template, and trials of paid tools like Pipedrive). Before choosing, check three things: how many users are free, how many contacts, and whether you can export your data to a spreadsheet if you ever leave.

Frequently asked questions

What’s the best free CRM for a sole trader?

It depends on how many of you there are. If you work alone, a well-built spreadsheet is often enough. If there are two of you and you want email history and connected forms, HubSpot’s free plan is a good starting point.

How many contacts does HubSpot allow for free?

According to its help centre, new free accounts can store up to 1,000 contacts and have up to 2 users. Older accounts may have other limits.

Can I use Excel or Google Sheets as a CRM?

Yes. With six columns (contact, source, status, next step, date and notes) and the habit of checking it every morning, a sheet works as a CRM for a small business.

When should I move from a spreadsheet to a CRM?

When several people deal with customers, when follow-ups slip even though the sheet is up to date, or when you’re copying by hand data that could come in automatically.
