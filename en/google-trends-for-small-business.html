Google Trends for your business: how to see what people near you search for

It is free and shows something a hunch cannot: when interest in what you sell goes up, and which words people use to ask for it. We explain it with two real queries from this week.

In short
  • Google Trends does not tell you how many searches there are. It gives interest from 0 to 100 against the highest point of the period and region you choose.
  • A business can use it for three things: which months people look for it, which words they use and in which region.
  • A one-day spike, like a football match, is almost never for you. A curve that comes back every year is.
  • One term on its own tells you nothing. Always compare two.

Most businesses decide what goes on their website and when to post by instinct. Google Trends shows what people actually type into the search box, by region and by date, and it costs nothing. This week we ran two queries to explain it.

What does Google Trends measure?

It measures interest, not volume. Each point on the graph is the share of searches for that term out of all searches in that place and moment, and then everything is scaled from 0 to 100, where 100 is the highest point of whatever you are looking at.

100The highest pointFor that term, in that period and that region. It does not mean "lots of people".
50Half the interestCompared with that peak. Change the dates and the scale changes.
0Very few searchesIt does not mean "none": Trends does not show low-volume terms.

That is why you cannot compare two graphs by eye. To compare, put both terms in the same query.

What does a 24-hour spike look like?

On Tuesday 24 November 2026 a Champions League match was played in Madrid at 21:00. We looked the match up in Trends, filtered by the Madrid region and the past 24 hours. It is the graph at the top of this article.

  1. Around 20:00Interest goes from almost zero to over 80 in a few minutes. People are checking where to watch it and when it starts.
  2. Just after 23:00It hits the peak, with the match just finished.
  3. At midnightIt is already down to a quarter.
  4. Between 5 and 7 in the morningIt climbs back to about half: those who went to bed early check the result.
  5. By mid-morningIt is back at zero.

Trends labels it a spike of more than 5,000% against the previous 24 hours. It sounds huge and it lasts less than a day. If you sell nothing related to the match, there is nothing to do with that spike. If you run a bar, there is: see how to fill your venue on match days. And if you run your business's social media, see when jumping on a trend is worth it.

Two lessons come out of this, and they apply to any business. The first has its own article: how to choose the keywords for your website.

Lesson 1The short word is not your customerSomeone typing "web" wants to open WhatsApp on a computer. Someone looking for an agency types "web design" and their city. The same goes for "hair" and "hairdresser".
Lesson 2What rises announces a seasonSki resort webcams climb in late November. Nobody announced it: you can see it in what people search for. Your business has its own version of this.

How do you use it in ten minutes?

  1. Type your service the way a customer would"Physio", not "advanced sports physiotherapy". Trends needs terms with plenty of searches.
  2. Filter by your region and by five yearsFive years shows whether the curve repeats every season.
  3. Add a second way of saying it"Physio" against "physiotherapist", "renovations" against "building work". You can compare up to five. The winner is the one people use, not the one you prefer.
  4. Scroll to "related queries" and look at "rising"These are the new questions. They give you headings for your site and ideas for your posts.
  5. Note the high monthsAnd get your website, your Google profile and your posts ready a month earlier, not the same week.

What do you do with each shape of curve?

What pays off for a small business is not what shoots up for a day. It is what comes back every year.

What does Google Trends not tell you?

It tells you
  • When interest rises and falls
  • Which of two words is used more
  • Which regions care most
  • Which new questions are appearing
It does not
  • How many searches there are
  • What happens in a small area: it shows zero
  • Whether those people buy
  • Whether your site shows up

For that last one there is Search Console and your profile's own statistics. We cover them in how to measure whether your website brings in customers and in the local SEO guide.

Frequently asked questions

Is Google Trends free?

Yes. You can use it at trends.google.com without paying and without creating an account.

Does Google Trends show how many searches a word gets?

No. It gives interest from 0 to 100 against the highest point of the chosen period and region. It is for comparing and spotting when interest rises, not for counting.

Is it useful for a small business in one city?

Yes, with general terms. You filter by region; in smaller areas or with very specific wording it often shows zero because there are too few searches.

How often should you check it?

Twice a year to review your wording, and a month before each busy season. There is no need to look every week.
