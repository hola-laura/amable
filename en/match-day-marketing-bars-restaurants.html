A match day is one of the few times you know weeks ahead when people will be looking for a place like yours. Even so, many venues only think about it that afternoon. This article is about having it ready.

When do people look for somewhere to watch it?

On Tuesday 24 November 2026 there was a Champions League match in Madrid at 21:00. In Google Trends, searches for the match in the Madrid region stayed close to zero all afternoon and shot up around 20:00, an hour before kick-off.

20:00 to 21:00The hour that decidesPeople look for where to watch. Anything not yet posted arrives too late.
During the matchThey are already seatedWhat you post now is for the next one.

How to read that graph, and make your own, is in Google Trends for your business.

What should your Google profile say?

Someone searching "bar showing the football" on their phone sees Google Maps profiles, not websites. Three things make the difference:

  1. An "Event" postGoogle lets you post updates, offers and events. An event has a title and start and end dates and times, and can show on your profile on mobile.
  2. That day's opening hoursIf the kitchen stays open later or you open earlier, set it as special hours. A venue that shows "closed" at 23:00 loses the booking.
  3. A photo of where you watchThe screen, the room, the terrace. No text on top and no club crests.

If the profile is not yet in your name, start with how to set up your Google Business Profile.

How do you take table bookings without answering the phone?

At eight in the evening nobody at the venue can pick up the phone. A booking link, on your profile and on Instagram, does that job. Ask for just four things:

Ask for
  • Name
  • Phone number
  • How many people
  • What time they arrive
Decide beforehand
  • How long you hold the table
  • Whether there is a minimum spend
  • How many tables you book and how many you keep free
  • What happens if fewer turn up

With an automatic confirmation and a reminder, the table does not sit empty. The rules are in online booking and reminders and how to reduce no-shows.

How do you tell your regulars?

WhatsApp BusinessA broadcast listOnly people who have agreed, and it only reaches those who have your number saved. One message per match, not three.
InstagramThe month's fixturesOne pinned post with every match you are showing, and a story on the day with the booking link.

More on the first in WhatsApp Business for small businesses.

What do you do each day?

  1. Three days beforePost the event on your profile and open bookings.
  2. That morningCheck the special hours and message your regulars.
  3. An hour beforeA story: "Four tables left". Only if it is true.
  4. The next dayAsk those who booked for a review and post the next match.

What can you not skip?

RightsA commercial licenceShowing football in a venue needs the commercial subscription from whichever broadcaster holds the rights. A home one does not cover premises open to the public.
Trade marksNo crests or official photosClub crests and competition logos are registered trade marks. Write the team names, the day and the time.
CapacityDo not promise what does not fitBook fewer tables than you have.

This is practical guidance, not legal advice: check your broadcaster's terms.

What if football is not your thing?

The method is the same for any date when you know demand is coming: office parties in December, Valentine's Day, a concert nearby. And it works the other way round: "No screens tonight" is a post too, and there are people looking for exactly that.

You do not need to post more. You need what you were already going to do written where people look.

Frequently asked questions

How far ahead should you post the match?

Three days before on your Google profile and Instagram, with a reminder on the day. Most searches come in the last hour, so it has to be posted before then.

Do you need a website to take table bookings?

Not strictly: a booking link on your Google profile and Instagram is enough. A website with its own booking avoids platform commissions.

Can I use the clubs' crests on the poster?

Better not. Club crests and competition logos are registered trade marks. The team names, the day and the time are enough.
