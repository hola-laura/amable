How to automate WhatsApp follow-ups without sounding like a robot

Someone messages you, you reply, and then… silence. Follow-up is where most customers are lost. You can automate part of it, but not with mass messages: here’s what to automate, what to write yourself and what the rules say.

In short
  • Follow-ups are lost through forgetfulness, not lack of interest. The fix is a system that reminds you, not one that messages everyone for you.
  • Automatic: the first acknowledgement, labels and internal reminders. Written by you: the real reply and every follow-up.

Let’s say you run a renovation business. On a Tuesday, three people message you on WhatsApp. You reply to one straight away, another in the evening, and the third… slips through. By Thursday you can’t remember who was waiting for a quote and who was just asking.

It isn’t a lack of interest: WhatsApp simply isn’t built to keep track. That’s the gap to fill.

Why do contacts go cold?

Because time works against you. In a study published in Harvard Business Review, companies that responded within the first hour were nearly seven times more likely to reach the decision-maker than those that waited an hour longer. And almost one in four of the companies studied never replied at all.

WhatsApp is no different, with one twist: people message several businesses at once. Whoever replies first, and then doesn’t disappear, usually gets the job.

What do WhatsApp’s rules and the law allow?

Before automating anything, three rules not to break:

24-hour windowFree repliesWith the API, you can reply freely for 24 hours after the customer’s last message. After that, only with templates approved by Meta.
PermissionOnly people who want itWhatsApp requires that the person gave you their number and agreed to receive your messages. Otherwise they can block or report you.
The lawMarketing needs consentIn Spain, the LSSI bans electronic marketing that wasn’t requested or authorised, and the regulator has fined WhatsApp sends. The UK and EU have similar rules.

Answering someone who asked you a question, and following up on it, isn’t marketing. Sending an offer to your whole contact list is.

What should you automate, and what not?

Automatic
  • The first acknowledgement (“I’ll reply within the hour”)
  • The out-of-hours message
  • Saving the contact to your sheet or CRM with the date
  • Reminding you who to message today
  • Appointment confirmations and reminders
Written by you
  • The answer to what they actually asked
  • Every follow-up, even if you start from a template
  • Price and terms
  • Any message after a “no”
  • Offers (and only to people who agreed)

The key is the last line on the left: the system doesn’t write for you, it reminds you. So every message sounds like a person, because it is one.

A four-step sequence

  1. Minute 0 · AutomaticA welcome or away message saying what happens next: “I’ll reply personally within the hour”. Label: “New”.
  2. First hour · YouThe real reply. If you need information, ask for one thing only (a photo, a date). Change the label to “Waiting for reply” or “Quote sent”.
  3. Day 2 · A reminder for youIf they haven’t replied, you get an alert. You write a short, useful message, never just “Hello?”.
  4. Day 7 · Friendly closeThe last one. Leave the door open and mark it “Closed”. There’s no fourth message.

Two follow-ups at most. Beyond that it isn’t following up, it’s chasing.

Messages to copy

Acknowledgement (automatic)

Hi! Thanks for getting in touch with [business]. I’ll reply personally within the hour (Monday to Friday, [time] to [time]). If you’d like to get ahead, tell me what you need and when.

Follow-up · day 2

Hi [name], did you get a chance to look at what I sent? If anything’s unclear about [what they asked for], I can answer here or we can have a quick 5-minute call.

Close · day 7

Hi [name], I don’t want to crowd you, so this is my last message. If it suits you later on, just message me and we’ll pick it up. All the best!

Notice that none of them asks “Are you still interested?”. That question makes the other person justify themselves. Better to offer something: an answer, a short call, a free slot.

Frequently asked questions

Can you automate follow-ups in WhatsApp Business?

Partly. The free app sends greeting and away messages on its own, and you manage the labels. With the API, reminders and approved template messages can be scheduled and connected to your sheet or CRM.

How many follow-up messages should I send?

Two at most: one after two days and a friendly close after a week. More than that tends to annoy people and can get you blocked.

Can I send promotions to my contacts on WhatsApp?

Only to people who agreed to receive them. WhatsApp requires prior permission, and in Spain the LSSI bans electronic marketing without consent.

What is WhatsApp’s 24-hour window?

It’s the period after the customer’s last message in which the API lets you reply freely. After that, you can only send templates approved by Meta.
