How to measure if your website brings customers: forms, calls, WhatsApp and bookings

“Is my website bringing in customers?” is the question asked most and answered with data least. You don’t need to be an analyst: just count the four doors people use to contact you and look at three figures a month.

In short
  • A service website brings customers through four doors: form, call, WhatsApp and booking. If you only count one, the site looks worse than it is.
  • In Google Analytics, each door is measured as an event: a submitted form with the recommended generate_lead event, plus clicks on call, WhatsApp or book.
  • Mark those events as key events to see them in reports, and remember that people who decline cookies aren’t counted the same way.
  • Each month, three figures: contacts, how many become customers and which channel they come from. Always ask “how did you hear about us?”.

Let’s say you run a tutoring academy. You open Google Analytics and see 600 visits a month and four forms. Conclusion: the website doesn’t work. But that month twelve new students signed up, and seven said they found you on Google. They called or messaged on WhatsApp from the website, and you weren’t counting that.

Measuring properly isn’t about more charts. It’s about counting every door.

The four doors of a service website

Door 1FormThe easiest to measure and often the least used.
Door 2CallThe “Call” button on a phone. Heavily used for urgent jobs and trades.
Door 3WhatsAppThe button that opens a chat. More common every year.
Door 4BookingThe link to your online calendar, if you take appointments.

What should you measure at each door?

How to do it in Google Analytics

Google Analytics 4 measures “events”, meaning things that happen on your site. Visits are measured automatically; the doors need setting up. The steps:

  1. One event per doorFor the form, Google recommends the generate_lead event, sent when someone submits a form or requests information. For buttons, an event on clicking call, WhatsApp or book.
  2. Better through Tag ManagerWith Google Tag Manager you set them up without editing the site each time. The site signals “form sent” and Tag Manager passes it on to Analytics.
  3. Mark them as key eventsIn Analytics, under Admin → Key events. They then show in reports as what matters, not lost among hundreds of events.
  4. Check it worksSend a form yourself and look for it in the Realtime report. Settings can take from a few minutes to a few hours to apply.

If you use the generate_lead event, Analytics also fills a lead acquisition report, showing the channel each lead came from.

What Analytics can’t see

It sees
  • Visits and where they come from (Google, Instagram, direct)
  • Forms sent and button clicks, if you set them up
  • Which pages people view before getting in touch
It doesn’t see
  • People who decline cookies (counted differently or not at all)
  • People who note your number and call later
  • People who call from your Google listing without visiting the site
  • Whether the contact became a customer

That’s why you need two more things. First, check the performance of your Google Business Profile, which counts calls and clicks from your listing. Second, the simplest and most revealing: ask every new customer “how did you hear about us?” and write it down.

Which three figures should you check each month?

1ContactsForms + calls + WhatsApp + bookings. The total across all four doors.
2CustomersHow many of those contacts ended up buying or booking.
3SourceWhich channel the people who become customers come from, not just those who ask.

A spreadsheet and ten minutes at the end of the month are enough. If every contact already arrives with its source in your sheet or CRM, it’s even easier: I explain it in how to connect your form, CRM, WhatsApp and calendar.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if my website brings me customers?

Add up contacts from all four doors (forms, calls, WhatsApp and bookings), ask every new customer how they found you, and compare each month how many contacts become customers and which channel they come from.

What is the generate_lead event in Google Analytics?

It’s a Google-recommended event sent when someone fills in a form or requests information. If you mark it as a key event, it appears in reports and in the lead acquisition report.

Can you measure calls and WhatsApp messages from a website?

Yes, as clicks on the call and WhatsApp buttons. It’s a good clue, but a click doesn’t guarantee the person actually called or wrote.

Why does Analytics show fewer visits than I actually get?

A common reason is the cookie banner: people who don’t accept analytics are counted in a limited way or not at all. That’s why it’s worth cross-checking with your calendar, your phone and your Google Business Profile.
