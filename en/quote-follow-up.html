How to automate quote follow-ups so none get lost

You send a quote, the customer says “I’ll have a look and let you know”, and you never hear back. Often it isn’t a no: nobody asked again. Here’s how to set up a follow-up that doesn’t rely on your memory.

In short
  • A quote with no reply is usually a forgotten quote, by you or the customer. A timely follow-up wins some of them back.
  • Every quote has a status (sent, undecided, won, lost, no reply) and a date for the next step.
  • The simplest timeline: send with an expiry date, ask after 3 days, last message after 10, then close.
  • What gets automated is your reminders. You write the message to the customer yourself, in two lines.

Let’s say you’re an installer. You do ten quotes a month, each one takes a visit and an afternoon of sums. Three say yes. Of the other seven, you know why you lost two. And the remaining five? Silence.

That silence is money. Not all of it can be recovered, but some can, and nearly always with something as simple as asking again at the right time.

Why do quotes get lost after you send them?

The most common reasons have nothing to do with price:

It slippedThey left it for laterThey opened it on their phone, thought “I’ll look at the weekend”, and the weekend filled up.
They have a questionAnd don’t ask itA line they don’t understand or a timeline that doesn’t suit. Silence is easier than asking.
They’re comparingWith two othersWhoever gets back in touch with something useful scores points.
You forgotAnd they noticeIf you don’t ask, it looks as if you don’t much care about the job.

Five statuses for every quote

First, you need to know where each one stands. Five statuses are enough, in a sheet or a CRM:

The follow-up timeline

  1. Day 0 · When you send itWith an expiry date (“Price valid until the 30th”) and a clear next step: “If it suits you, a deposit secures the date”. Log it as “Sent”.
  2. Day 3 · First check-inShort and useful: offer to answer questions, don’t ask “what did you think?”.
  3. Day 10 · Last messageMention the expiry date and leave the door open. No pressure.
  4. Day 14 · CloseIf there’s no reply, it becomes “No reply”. Out of your head.

Adjust the days to your trade: a big renovation takes longer to decide than a repair. What matters is fixed days, not “when I remember”.

Messages to copy

When sending

Hi [name], here’s the quote for [job]. The price is valid until [date]. If it suits you, a deposit secures [start date]. And if anything’s unclear, just ask.

Day 3

Hi [name], did any questions come up about the quote? Happy to go through it on a 5-minute call and explain any line.

Day 10

Hi [name], this is my last message about the quote for [job]: the price holds until [date]. If it doesn’t go ahead, no problem; it would help me to know why, so I can improve.

How to automate it

What gets automated isn’t the message to the customer: it’s your reminders. Three ways, from simplest to most complete:

SimpleSheet + daily alertA sheet with status and date. Every morning you get an email listing the quotes due today.
MiddleCRM with tasksWhen a quote moves to “Sent”, the day-3 and day-10 tasks are created automatically.
CompleteYour own toolYou write the quote, it’s sent, logged and you’re reminded, all in one place.

Ask why you lose

The last message asks for the reason, and not just to be polite. If you log why people say no, within a few months you’ll know whether you lose on price, on timing or on how you explain the quote. Then you can improve what actually matters.

Frequently asked questions

When should you follow up on a quote?

A first message 2 or 3 days after sending it and a last one around day 10. If there’s no reply, close it as “no reply” and don’t chase further.

What should you say when following up on a quote?

Offer something useful: answering questions or going through it on a short call. Avoid “what did you think?” or “are you still interested?”, which make people justify themselves.

Can you automate quote follow-ups?

Yes. The most useful part to automate is your reminders, from a sheet or a CRM, while you write the message to the customer from a template.

Should a quote have an expiry date?

It’s highly recommended. It gives a clear date to decide by and a natural reason for the last follow-up message.
