How to use AI to sort customer feedback and decide what to improve

You have reviews, WhatsApp messages, emails and what people tell you at the counter. Reading it all together is what there is never time for. AI sorts it in minutes, if you give it the material properly and check what it gives back.

In short
  • Put the feedback in a single document, one comment per line, with the date and where each one came from.
  • Before giving it to an AI, remove names, phone numbers and anything that identifies a person.
  • Ask for themes with the number of mentions and two or three verbatim quotes. If a theme has no quotes, it does not exist.
  • Rank by frequency, impact and effort, and turn the top two or three into an action with a way of knowing whether it worked.

When I was designing for a bank, a large part of the job was understanding what the people using the product were saying. The hard part was never getting feedback. It was reading it all together and seeing what kept coming up.

A small business has the same problem on a different scale. And here AI really helps, because summarising and grouping text is exactly what it does well.

Where does the feedback come from?

PublicReviewsGoogle, social media, platforms in your sector. The good and the bad.
PrivateMessagesWhatsApp, emails, the website form. Above all, the questions that repeat.
SpokenWhat people tell youAt the counter or on the phone. Write it down the same day, in their words.

Copy it all into one document, one comment per line, with the month and the source. Fifty is enough to get something useful. If you have few reviews, start with how to get more Google reviews.

What should you remove before giving it to an AI?

Anything that identifies a person. The Spanish Data Protection Agency (AEPD) recommends not sharing personal data (full name, address, phone number, ID number) or sensitive information, such as medical or financial details, with these tools.

  • First names and surnames: change them to “customer 1”, “customer 2”.
  • Phone numbers, emails and addresses: delete them.
  • Details about health, money or family: remove whatever is not needed to understand the complaint.

To know what to improve, you do not need to know who said it. Only what they said.

How do I know it hasn’t made it up?

  1. Look up every quoteIt has to be in your document exactly as written. If it is not there, discard the whole theme.
  2. Count two themes by handIf it says “14 mentions” and you find 6, ask it to redo it or count it yourself.
  3. Read “Other”The most interesting things sometimes hide there: what only one person said, and is serious.
  4. Do it twiceIf very different themes come out the second time, the material is thin or confusing.
AI sorts. Deciding what matters is still up to you.

How do you decide what goes first?

Score each theme from 1 to 3 on three things and add them up:

FrequencyHow many people say it?One case: 1. Several: 2. Again and again: 3.
ImpactWhat does it cost you?An annoyance: 1. The odd sale: 2. Customers who do not come back: 3.
EaseHow hard is it to fix?Months or a lot of money: 1. A few weeks: 2. This week: 3.

The two or three themes with the most points are your list. Not ten: two or three.

How do you turn a theme into an action you can check?

In three months, repeat the exercise with the new feedback. If the theme has gone, it worked. To measure what happens on the website, see how to measure whether your website brings in customers.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use ChatGPT or Claude to analyse customer reviews?

Yes. Grouping and summarising text is among the things they do best. Remove personal data first, ask for verbatim quotes for each theme and check that those quotes are in your document.

How much feedback do you need?

With about fifty comments, patterns start to show. You can do it with fewer, but treat the result as clues, not conclusions.

Is it legal to give customer feedback to an AI?

It depends on what it contains. The prudent approach is to remove anything that identifies a person before using it. The Spanish Data Protection Agency recommends not sharing personal data or sensitive information with these tools. If you are unsure about your case, ask whoever handles your data protection.

How often should you repeat it?

Every three months is a good rhythm for a small business: it gives changes time to show and new feedback time to arrive.
