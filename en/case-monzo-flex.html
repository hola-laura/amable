From “pay later” to credit card

I led design on Monzo Flex as it stopped being a pay-later product and became a credit card, working with two product squads, research, marketing, legal and risk.

The challenge

In early 2023, Flex was 15% behind its customer goal. People saw it as “buy now, pay later” and felt it wasn’t meant for them.

The account was also built purchase by purchase. 37% of customers had more than ten active purchases, so changing their monthly payment meant editing each one.

What I did

  1. Listen first

    A survey of 1,924 customers who had never used Flex, 20 interviews and a product audit. 65% had used credit cards; only 9% had used pay later.

  2. Change the message

    Position Flex as a credit card. The travel message brought more applications but fewer opened accounts. In a test with 870,000 customers, the new message opened 20% more accounts.

  3. One account, not a list

    I redesigned the account so people could see and pay their whole balance, with one amount split across all purchases.

  4. Fewer missed payments, same control

    I proposed removing “pay in full” as the default and testing “choose for every purchase” first. Less risk of failed payments, and the choice stays with the customer.

  5. Test with people

    In the second round of testing, 9 out of 10 people completed the tasks unaided.

Results

+37%retention
−15%customers missing payments
−20.5%payment complaints
1M+Flex customers by March 2025

These are results for the whole team, not design alone.

What I learned

Risk and business belong in the first workshop. And success is measured by how many people keep up with payments, not only by sign-ups.

For your business

Design for products where a mistake costs money: decide with data, test with people and measure what matters.
